## 01:08  How Empirical Bayesian Kriging works
**[01:09.4]** Empirical Bayesian Kriging uses spatial relationships among observed measurements to estimate patterns between those observations.  
**[01:18.6]** Through repeated simulations, it also accounts for uncertainty in the fitted spatial correlation model.  
**[01:27.3]** The result is an estimated surface, not a record of infections in locations without observations.  

## 01:36  What a surface cannot tell us
**[01:36.8]** Our spatial maps helped us explore patterns in the available data.  
**[01:41.1]** But they could not tell us where infections had gone undetected.  
**[01:45.3]** An estimated hotspot is not a confirmed outbreak.  

## 01:50  Conceptual application: Ebola preparedness
**[01:50.9]** Consider Ebola preparedness in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and neighboring areas.  
**[01:55.9]** Could spatial estimates, combined with reliable surveillance, population, mobility, and testing information, help teams decide where to investigate more closely?  
**[02:05.5]** Potentially.  
**[02:06.9]** But the estimates would need validation and local epidemiological interpretation before informing action.
